Hi there! 😎
------
My name is <span style="color: #BA68C8;">Youngdong Cho</span> (pronounced as [jʌŋdoŋ dʑo]), a fourth-year PhD student in the <a href="https://linguistics.cornell.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Department of Linguistics</a> at <a href="https://www.cornell.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cornell University</a>, minoring in <a href="https://as.cornell.edu/major_minor_gradfield/cognitive-science" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cognitive Science</a>.


My primary research interests lie in theoretical semantics and its interface with syntax and pragmatics. One of my ongoing projects examines the semantics and pragmatics of non-canonical questions focusing on Korean. My research interests also lie in experimental and computational approaches to linguistic phenomena. I am currently working with <a href="https://linguistics.cornell.edu/sarah-murray" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Sarah Murray</a> and <a href="https://linguistics.cornell.edu/john-b-whitman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">John Whitman</a>.


Prior to coming to Cornell, I received my M.A. in English Language and Literature at Seoul National University, where I worked on the syntax and semantics of fragments in English echo questions. I hold a B.A. from the same institution, with a minor in Linguistics.
